The Problem

Enterprise ML Has a Governance Gap - Not a Model Gap

Most organizations have data, models, and experiments. What they are missing is the governed infrastructure that connects those signals to commercially accountable decisions.

Enterprise marketing, retention, and revenue teams share a common frustration. The data exists. The models run. The experiments happen. But the decisions that should follow - the campaign to launch, the customer to retain, the budget to reallocate, the channel to cut - are delayed, inconsistent, or never happen at all.

The problem is not the model. The problem is everything around the model.

Model Outputs Without Context

Data scientists present churn scores at a business review. The audience asks: which customers, what actions, what confidence, by when? Nobody has the answer. The model never reaches production.

Attribution Without Causality

Marketing reports channel performance using last-touch or multi-touch attribution. The real question - what actually caused this conversion - is never answered. Budget flows to channels that correlate with revenue but do not cause it.

Recommendations Without Activation

The Next Best Action model runs and produces a ranked list. Nobody knows how to translate that list into a CRM update, campaign segment, or customer communication. The model lives in a dashboard.

Governance as a Final Checkpoint

Data quality, model readiness, and lineage are reviewed once, right before deployment. Problems surface at the worst possible moment, fixes are expensive, and release cycles slow.

These are not edge cases. They repeat in almost every enterprise ML deployment across every industry. They share a root cause: ML platforms are built to produce model outputs, not to govern the path from data to decision.

How It Works

A Contract-Driven Platform That Connects Data to Governed Decisions

Four use cases. Eight pipeline stages. One evidence-first architecture. Every run is auditable from source data to business action.

This platform is built around a single principle: governance is not a checkpoint at the end of a pipeline. It is an output of every stage. Every use case is defined by a contract that specifies inputs, outputs, runtime model selection, fallback behavior, and governance requirements.

Use Cases

Four Production Use Cases. Three Distinct ML Paradigms.

Different business problems require different kinds of answers. This platform covers discriminative, Bayesian, and causal ML.

UC-NBA-RET-001 - Next Best Action for Retention

Business question: Which action should we take with this customer right now to maximize retention?

ML approach: TensorFlow uplift modeling and action ranking.

Output: Ranked action list with expected impact, confidence interval, customer segment context, and CRM-ready activation payload.

Why: Churn tells you who is at risk. Uplift tells you what intervention can change the outcome for each customer.

UC-CHURN-RET-002 - Churn Prediction and Retention Actioning

Business question: Which customers are at risk, at what probability, and with what urgency?

ML approach: TensorFlow classifier trained on behavioral, usage, support, and commercial signals.

Output: Churn probability, confidence bands, signal breakdown, urgency tier, and recommended retention motion.

Why: The probability distribution matters more than a binary flag; 78% risk and 52% risk require different treatment.

UC-MMM-PLN-003 - Media Mix Modeling and Budget Optimization

Business question: What is the true contribution of each channel, and how should budget move?

ML approach: PyMC-Marketing Bayesian hierarchical model.

Output: Channel contribution distributions, budget recommendations, uncertainty bounds, saturation curves, and diminishing return estimates.

Why: Bayesian MMM shows both what the model estimates and how confident it is, which matters for budget decisions.

UC-INCR-MKT-004 - Campaign Incrementality Measurement

Business question: Did this campaign actually cause the outcome, or would those customers have converted anyway?

ML approach: EconML and DoWhy causal inference.

Output: Incremental lift, average treatment effect, confidence intervals, counterfactual baseline, and channel-level incrementality.

Why: Causal inference estimates whether the campaign changed behavior, which is the budget question that matters.

DiscriminativeTensorFlow for classification, ranking, propensity, and uplift.
BayesianPyMC-Marketing for attribution, budget optimization, and uncertainty.
CausalEconML + DoWhy for incrementality, counterfactuals, and policy decisions.

Governance

Evidence-First Governance - Not Compliance Theater

Every pipeline run produces inspectable evidence at every stage. Governance is an artifact, not an approval email.

Stage 2 - IngestionData quality validation runs before any model sees the data. Blockers and warnings are recorded in the artifact manifest.
Stage 4 - Identity ResolutionSplink probabilistic matching records match rates, confidence thresholds, and resolution decisions.
Stage 5 - Feature LayerFeature snapshots are versioned per run, point-in-time correctness is enforced, and schema versions are tracked.
Stage 6 - Model LayerReadiness checks, evaluation metrics, champion/challenger comparison, and registry metadata are recorded before promotion.
Stage 7 - Serving + ActivationInference produces activation payloads traceable to model version, feature snapshot, and training run.
Stage 8 - Monitoring + GovernancePrometheus metrics, MLflow lineage, and governance approvals track runtime health and promotion readiness.
Promotion lifecycle: generate synthetic data, train versioned artifacts, evaluate baseline versus challenger, run model-readiness checks, register metadata, approve warnings, promote to approved status, and deploy to serving.

Audit Trail

One run ID connects every artifact from source data to business action: source snapshot hash, feature vector snapshot, model version, prediction confidence, activation mapping, telemetry, governance approval, and run summary manifest.

Architecture

Three ML Paradigms. Eight Pipeline Stages. One Contract-Driven Platform.

The technology choices are deliberate. Each framework solves the problem type it is best suited for.

TensorFlow

Used for NBA and Churn because these are supervised learning problems: uplift modeling, multi-class classification, action ranking, and propensity scoring.

PyMC-Marketing

Used for Media Mix Modeling because MMM is fundamentally about uncertainty quantification, channel contribution distributions, and budget decisions under uncertainty.

EconML + DoWhy

Used for Campaign Incrementality because the platform must answer whether an intervention changed outcomes, for which customers, and by how much.

Runtime Profiles

Standalone, Advanced ML, OSS Integrated, and Enterprise profiles scale infrastructure while preserving the same contract outputs, governance artifacts, and audit trail.

Feast feature management MLflow registry Splink identity resolution Kafka event bus PostgreSQL + MinIO storage Airflow orchestration Keycloak access control Prometheus monitoring
Design principles: contract before code, evidence before promotion, fallback without silence, and infrastructure by injection.
